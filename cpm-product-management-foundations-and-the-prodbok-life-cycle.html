Question 1 of 6 · Product Management Foundations and the ProdBOK Life Cycle
A product has formally passed all Qualify-phase test exit criteria, but beta customer feedback surfaces a critical usability issue that was not covered by the original exit criteria. According to ProdBOK gate-review practice, what should the gatekeeper decide?
Stage-gate/phase-gate models define four standard gate outcomes — Go, Kill, Hold, Recycle. When new information (like an unanticipated critical defect) surfaces that isn't addressed by the original criteria, Recycle is the correct response: the item returns to an earlier phase with a defined action plan rather than being auto-approved or terminated.
Question 2 of 6 · Product Management Foundations and the ProdBOK Life Cycle
In an agile software team, one person breaks epics into sprint-ready user stories, sits with developers daily, and prioritizes the sprint backlog based on strategic direction set upstream by another role who owns the business case, roadmap, and market positioning across the full life cycle. Which ProdBOK role does the first person occupy?
ProdBOK distinguishes the Product Owner as the tactical, execution-focused role embedded with the delivery team — writing/prioritizing backlog items and enabling day-to-day development — operating within direction set by the strategic Product Manager role.
Question 3 of 6 · Product Management Foundations and the ProdBOK Life Cycle
ProdBOK describes the product manager as the internal 'go-to' person for the product. Which activity BEST exemplifies this internal-facing 'go-to' coordination role, as distinct from outbound, market-facing duties?
The 'go-to' function is explicitly an inbound/internal coordination responsibility — internal teams direct product-related questions to the PM, who provides authoritative direction on priorities and product decisions.
Question 4 of 6 · Product Management Foundations and the ProdBOK Life Cycle
According to the ProdBOK Seven-Phase Product Life Cycle, which activity is MOST characteristic of the Retire phase?
Retire is the final of the seven phases, focused on formally sunsetting a product — defining end-of-life timelines, migration paths for existing customers, and final support obligations before withdrawal from the market.
Question 5 of 6 · Product Management Foundations and the ProdBOK Life Cycle
In a stage-gate/phase-gate implementation, which of the following BEST describes the primary basis on which a gatekeeper approves passage from one phase to the next?
Stage-gate models are built around defined deliverables and exit criteria for each gate; the gatekeeper's role is to objectively evaluate whether those specific, pre-established criteria have been met before authorizing the next phase.
Question 6 of 6 · Product Management Foundations and the ProdBOK Life Cycle
A company has completed a detailed feasibility study, business case, and initial product requirements document, and is now assembling cross-functional resources and finalizing the project charter before development begins. Which phase of the Seven-Phase Product Life Cycle is this?
Plan is the phase where the business case, feasibility work, and requirements are solidified and resourcing/charter activities occur to prepare for execution — exactly the activities described.
