Question 1 of 6 · Business strategies for a successful gen AI solution
A manufacturing company has identified three potential gen AI use cases: predictive maintenance (estimated $2M annual savings, 18-month implementation), automated quality inspection ($800K annual savings, 6-month implementation), and customer support chatbot ($1.2M annual savings, 9-month implementation). The company has budget for only one pilot and leadership needs to demonstrate quick ROI to secure funding for broader adoption. Which prioritization approach BEST aligns with business strategy principles for gen AI solution success?
Quality inspection offers the fastest time-to-value (6 months) with measurable ROI ($800K/year), enabling the organization to demonstrate success quickly, build confidence, secure additional funding, and establish best practices before tackling more complex use cases. Quick wins are essential for organizational adoption and sustained investment in gen AI.
Question 2 of 6 · Business strategies for a successful gen AI solution
An insurance company's gen AI chatbot pilot achieved 85% customer satisfaction but is being blocked from production deployment by the Chief Risk Officer due to concerns about hallucinations in policy explanations. The development team proposes switching from a pure LLM to a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) architecture grounded in the company's verified policy database with human-in-the-loop review for complex cases. Which governance principle does this solution PRIMARILY address?
RAG with human-in-the-loop directly addresses the CRO's hallucination risk concern by grounding responses in verified policy data (reducing fabrication) and adding human review for complex cases where errors have highest business impact. This demonstrates responsible AI governance through accountability mechanisms and risk reduction—a core leadership responsibility in gen AI deployment.
Question 3 of 6 · Business strategies for a successful gen AI solution
A retail organization has successfully piloted a gen AI product description generator for 500 SKUs, reducing content creation time by 70%. The CMO wants to scale to 50,000 SKUs but finance projects that Vertex AI API costs will increase from $2,000/month to $180,000/month at that scale, exceeding budget. Which cost-awareness strategy should the AI leader recommend to enable scaling while managing expenses?
This approach combines multiple cost-optimization strategies (enterprise pricing, technical efficiency via batching/caching, right-sizing models to task complexity) while enabling full-scale deployment. It demonstrates strategic cost management that maintains business value and aligns technical decisions with budget constraints—essential for sustainable gen AI scaling.
Question 4 of 6 · Business strategies for a successful gen AI solution
A healthcare provider's AI leadership team is measuring ROI for a gen AI clinical documentation assistant deployed to 200 physicians. After 6 months, time savings average 45 minutes per physician per day, but patient visit volume has not increased and physician survey data shows 30% report using the saved time for administrative tasks rather than patient care. Leadership questions whether the $500K investment is delivering strategic value. How should the AI leader reframe ROI measurement?
This approach demonstrates mature business impact measurement by recognizing that healthcare ROI includes workforce sustainability (burnout reduction), quality improvements, and strategic enablers (after-hours reduction, satisfaction) beyond direct revenue. Physician retention, reduced errors, and work-life improvements have substantial long-term value that narrow productivity metrics miss—essential for defending AI investments in complex domains.
Question 5 of 6 · Business strategies for a successful gen AI solution
A financial services firm is developing a gen AI investment recommendation system. The Chief Compliance Officer requires that every recommendation include an explanation of the reasoning and identify which data sources influenced the output, and that the system maintains an audit trail of all recommendations for regulatory review. Which responsible AI governance capability is MOST critical to implement?
Explainability (showing reasoning), data lineage (tracking sources), and audit logging directly address the CCO's regulatory requirements for transparency and accountability in financial recommendations. These capabilities enable regulatory compliance, risk management, and oversight—core governance responsibilities when deploying gen AI in regulated industries.
Question 6 of 6 · Business strategies for a successful gen AI solution
A telecommunications company has deployed gen AI for network optimization and customer service, with both systems accessing customer data. Six months post-deployment, a data privacy audit reveals that the customer service gen AI has been inadvertently exposing call detail records in chat logs, creating GDPR violation risk. The network optimization system, which uses the same data, has proper privacy controls. Which organizational change management principle did leadership MOST likely fail to implement?
The fact that one system had proper privacy controls while another didn't—despite using the same data—indicates lack of unified governance standards and compliance review processes. Cross-functional governance ensures consistent privacy, security, and compliance practices across all gen AI deployments, preventing siloed implementations with varying risk management maturity.
