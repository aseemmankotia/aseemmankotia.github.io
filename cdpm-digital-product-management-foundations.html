Question 1 of 6 · Digital Product Management Foundations
A digital product team ships a new onboarding flow every two weeks and reports '12 features shipped, 8 sprints completed' to leadership as evidence of success. Leadership questions whether the product is actually succeeding. What should the PM change first?
CDPM's core outcome-orientation principle requires evidence tied to customer/business results (activation, retention) rather than counts of shipped items, which is the definition of output-based reporting.
Question 2 of 6 · Digital Product Management Foundations
A company is moving from an IT-project funding model (a fixed 9-month project with an approved budget and a go-live date) to a persistent product team model. Which change most reflects an authentic shift to a product operating model, rather than surface-level relabeling?
The defining shift in a product operating model is funding and team structure moving from project-based (fixed scope, budget, and end date) to product-based (persistent teams funded against ongoing outcomes).
Question 3 of 6 · Digital Product Management Foundations
Multiple product teams each independently build their own authentication logic, causing duplicated effort and inconsistent security practices across the digital product portfolio. What is the most appropriate structural response?
CDPM's platform-product concept treats a shared, reusable capability (like authentication) as an internal product with its own API and internal customers, eliminating duplication while preserving the autonomy of consuming teams.
Question 4 of 6 · Digital Product Management Foundations
A digital subscription product has been live for three years and is in a mature, stable growth phase. Unlike a physical product's 'maturity' stage, where efforts typically focus on defending market share until an eventual decline, how should a digital PM appropriately run the product at this stage?
Digital products don't follow a fixed physical-product decline curve; the exam tests that continuous delivery and discovery persist indefinitely, allowing mature products to keep compounding outcome improvements through ongoing experimentation.
Question 5 of 6 · Digital Product Management Foundations
Which statement most accurately distinguishes the digital product life cycle from a traditional physical product life cycle, as tested on the CDPM exam?
This is the exam's core definitional distinction: physical products move through introduction-growth-maturity-decline stages toward a final discontinuation, while digital products remain in perpetual delivery, discovery, and improvement cycles post-launch.
Question 6 of 6 · Digital Product Management Foundations
A company's data science team builds an internal analytics dashboard used by five other product teams to make pricing decisions. Leadership asks whether this dashboard should be managed as a 'data product.' What is the strongest justification for treating it as a product?
CDPM defines a product by the presence of identifiable users/customers, evolving needs, and ongoing value delivery through iteration — internal platforms and data products qualify even without external revenue, as long as these characteristics are present.
