Question 2 of 6 · Google Cloud's generative AI offerings
A healthcare research organization is comparing AI infrastructure options for training large multimodal models that combine medical imaging with clinical notes. They need to process 50TB of training data, require maximum performance per dollar, and want to leverage Google's latest AI-optimized hardware. Their ML team is experienced with TensorFlow and PyTorch. What is the PRIMARY advantage of choosing TPU v5e over GPU-based infrastructure on Google Cloud for this workload?
TPU v5e (and Google's TPU family) are custom-designed ASICs specifically optimized for the matrix multiplication and tensor operations that dominate transformer model training and inference. Google Cloud's TPUs deliver better performance-per-dollar specifically for AI workloads compared to general-purpose GPUs because they're purpose-built for these operations. For large-scale model training with massive datasets (50TB), the cost-performance advantage of TPUs is the primary technical and business driver for choosing them over GPU infrastructure.

Question 6 of 6 · Google Cloud's generative AI offerings
A pharmaceutical company is building an AI application that generates drug interaction summaries for clinicians. Responses must cite current medical literature and FDA guidance, with verifiable sources. The development team is debating between two approaches: (A) using Gemini with Google Search grounding, or (B) using Gemini with enterprise data grounding connected to their licensed medical database subscriptions (UpToDate, PubMed institutional access). From a Google Cloud architecture perspective, what is the MOST important technical distinction between these grounding approaches?
This correctly identifies the fundamental architectural and functional difference between the two grounding approaches. Google Search grounding queries the public web, providing broad access to recent information but without control over sources or quality. Enterprise data grounding (through Vertex AI Search) connects to specific data sources the organization explicitly configures—licensed databases, internal documents, approved knowledge bases—giving the organization complete control over what information the model can cite. For pharmaceutical/clinical applications requiring verifiable, authoritative sources (licensed medical databases), enterprise data grounding is essential because it ensures responses cite only trusted, controlled sources rather than arbitrary web content.
