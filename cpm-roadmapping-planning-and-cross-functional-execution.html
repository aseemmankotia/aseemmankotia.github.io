Question 1 of 6 · Roadmapping, Planning, and Cross-Functional Execution
Six weeks before a committed GA date, engineering informs the product manager that the new feature depends on a shared design-system component that the design team has not yet scheduled to build, and marketing has already begun external launch communications tied to the original date. Per AIPMM front-end life cycle practice, what is the BEST next action?
ProdBOK treats dependency and risk management as ongoing, visible artifacts (dependency maps, risk registers) that feed structured cross-functional review. The PM's role as cross-functional 'go-to' is to surface the risk early, quantify the impact via the dependency map, and bring the affected functions together to renegotiate scope/timeline before external commitments (marketing communications) make the problem harder to unwind.
Question 2 of 6 · Roadmapping, Planning, and Cross-Functional Execution
A product organization must prioritize 40 backlog items spanning three product lines. Senior stakeholders require that each item be traceable to a strategic theme and an estimated business value, but engineering can only supply rough effort tiers (Small/Medium/Large) rather than precise estimates. Which prioritization approach BEST fits this constraint set?
A weighted scoring model can incorporate multiple criteria — including strategic theme alignment and business value — and tolerates coarse (tiered) effort inputs, making it defensible to senior stakeholders while working within the team's actual estimation capability.
Question 3 of 6 · Roadmapping, Planning, and Cross-Functional Execution
A product line faces high uncertainty in evolving customer requirements, but has a fixed budget and a hard regulatory compliance deadline that cannot move. Which planning approach BEST balances these constraints?
Rolling wave planning lets teams plan the near term in detail while keeping later work at a lower fidelity, accommodating requirements uncertainty. Embedding this inside a phase-gated structure preserves the fixed regulatory milestones and budget as non-negotiable gates, satisfying both constraints simultaneously.
Question 4 of 6 · Roadmapping, Planning, and Cross-Functional Execution
Which of the following BEST describes a required component set of a release plan used to coordinate cross-functional go-to-market readiness (sales, support, marketing)?
ProdBOK's release planning practice ties execution artifacts (features, dependencies) directly to cross-functional readiness — sales and support need enablement/training timelines and marketing needs launch metrics defined in advance so all functions synchronize around the same release plan rather than working from fragmented sources.
Question 5 of 6 · Roadmapping, Planning, and Cross-Functional Execution
In the AIPMM front-end life cycle framework, how does a product roadmap differ from a project plan for a specific initiative?
ProdBOK distinguishes the roadmap as a strategic communication tool — themes, priorities, and rough time horizons for stakeholders — from the project plan, which is the tactical execution document containing task-level detail, resource assignments, dependencies, and schedules for delivering a specific roadmap item.
Question 6 of 6 · Roadmapping, Planning, and Cross-Functional Execution
During a quarterly roadmap review, the VP of Sales insists on adding an unplanned feature to secure a key account renewal, while the executive sponsor wants the roadmap to stay focused on the previously agreed strategic themes. What is the BEST way for the product manager to resolve this conflict?
Stakeholder alignment in ProdBOK relies on transparent, criteria-based processes rather than authority or avoidance. Running the request through the same intake/prioritization framework used for other items and presenting a trade-off analysis lets the steering group make an informed decision, preserving both stakeholder trust and roadmap integrity.
